TypeVoice vs Wispr Flow

Both turn speech into typed text with a hotkey. They differ on one thing that matters more than the rest: where your voice goes. As of September 2026; tell us if something's out of date.

TypeVoiceWispr Flow
Where speech is recognisedOn your Mac (Neural Engine). Nothing is uploaded.In the cloud. Their docs say transcription always happens on their servers.
Works offlineYes, always.No.
AccountNone. No sign-up, no email, no login.Required.
Price$79 once. 7-day trial, no card.$12–15 a month, or a limited free tier.
Source codeOpen, GPL v3.Closed.
Cleanup of ums, stutters, self-correctionsBy rule, plus Apple Intelligence on this Mac.Their models in the cloud; broader edits, more languages.
LanguagesEnglish, every accent.100+ languages.
PlatformsmacOS 26 on Apple silicon.Mac, Windows, iPhone, Android.
Personal dictionaryYes, matched by how a word sounds.Yes.
Send the voice, not just the wordsDrag any dictation into iMessage, Slack, WhatsApp as a voice note.No.
A note on the end of every dictationYes. Add a line like “(dictated; spellings may be off)” to everything you say, so a coding assistant or a ticket queue reads an odd spelling as speech, not a typo, and doesn’t go chasing it.No.
TeamsOne key, 5 people, $299 once.Per seat, per month.

When Wispr Flow is the better choice

You dictate in a language other than English, you need the same tool on Windows or your phone, or you want the most aggressive AI rewriting and don't mind that it happens on someone else's computer.

When TypeVoice is

You write things you wouldn't want on a server: client work, medical or legal notes, code, anything under NDA, or just your own life. You'd rather pay once than every month. You want to be able to read the code that's listening to you. You're on a Mac and you speak English.
